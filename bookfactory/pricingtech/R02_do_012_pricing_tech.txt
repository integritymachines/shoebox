== T'61 vs proposal ==
v025 L2790: "In the statute, price discrimination means only a price difference between purchasers; the difference is unlawful where its effect may be to harm competition in one of those ways and neither defense applies. That is not the economic sense Discrimination in Segmentation took from Pigou, in which prices differ across buyers or quantities with willingness to pay rather than cost, and the two meanings are often confused."
Word for word identical to the T'61 proposal in R02_do_011. No deviation.

== Antecedents ==
"neither defense": the preceding sentence (same line L2790) reads "it allows defenses for differences justified by cost and for prices set in good faith to meet a competitor's offer". It names exactly two defenses, cost justification and meeting competition in good faith, so "neither" (two-way) resolves unambiguously.
"one of those ways": refers to the three effect forms in "substantially to lessen competition, tend to create a monopoly, or injure competition with the seller, the favored buyer, or their customers". It resolves, and "its effect" now agrees in number with "the effect may be".

== Legal accuracy ==
- Definition ("only a price difference") is correct (Anheuser-Busch 1960).
- "may be" probability standard is correct (13(a); Morton Salt).
- The defenses as a bar to unlawfulness are correct (13(a) cost proviso; 13(b) meeting competition, a complete defense per Standard Oil 1951).
- Residual point: 13(a) also contains a changing-conditions proviso (price changes in response to changing market conditions, such as deterioration of perishables, obsolescence, distress or closing-out sales). Courts and commentators often count it as a third defense. The text does not say the two are exhaustive, but "neither defense applies" reads as if they were. Verdict: Interpretative(Plausible), non-blocking. Optional T'62.
- Previous Implausible flag (L2789/2790 "unlawful when it has those effects") is cleared.
